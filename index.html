Entries 1–4

The economic transformation

From the failing galleon system to export agriculture, land, and ports.

01 · Changing economy

The old system breaks

By the late 1700s, the Galleon Trade was already failing. Basco's answer was the Royal Philippine Company, founded in 1785 to bankroll agriculture and open new markets across Asia and Europe.

"As the Philippine economy hung in the balance, Manila was opened to world trade by 1834."

The Mexican War of Independence began in 1810, the Galleon Trade ended in 1815, and Manila opened fully to world trade by 1834.

02 · Ports & regions

Beyond Manila

Iloilo, Cebu, and other ports pulled more of the archipelago into the export economy. Important commercial regions included Iloilo, Negros, Batangas, Pampanga, and Camarines.

04 · Land, debt, and tenancy

How a smallholder could become a tenant

I A smallholder needs capital and sells land under a pacto de retroventa, keeping the right to buy it back.
II Rising land demand makes the buy-back price harder to meet as the pact is renewed.
III Debt accumulates faster than the farmer can repay it.
IV The land is forfeited; the former owner becomes a kasama, a tenant on his former land.

Land grabbing added pressure, while the inquilino rented land in bulk and sublet it to smaller farmers.

05 · The Chinese

"Necessary outsiders"

Trade with China predated Spanish rule. Chinese residents worked as merchants, shopkeepers, artisans, traders, agricultural entrepreneurs, and moneylenders, while also facing higher taxes, forced residence in the Parian, and periodic expulsions.

06 · Chinese mestizo

A rising economic group

Chinese mestizos built wealth through agriculture, landownership, trade, retail, leasing farmland, and export enterprises.

Rizal's family in Calamba was economically established and involved in agriculture — a position that helped provide him with access to education.

Entries 8–10

Life under the new order

Education, urban change, colonial record-keeping, travel, and uneven prosperity.

08 · Education

Schools and a literate workforce

In 1836, the colonial government ordered every town to open a primary school. Schools such as Ateneo Municipal helped train the growing professional class.

08 · Urban life

Manila's prosperity had costs

Urban growth brought crowding, strained housing, sanitation problems, more crime, and a shifting tax base.

08 · Travel

The Suez Canal opens

When the Suez Canal opened in 1869, travel between the Philippines and Europe shortened dramatically, making study abroad easier.

09–10 · Ilustrados & uneven gains

A new educated middle class

Economic growth created a Filipino middle class from landownership, agriculture, trade, business, government posts, and professional work. Families able to educate their children in Manila or Europe produced the ilustrados.

At the same time, prosperity was uneven. Many ordinary Filipinos continued to face taxation, forced labor obligations, land disputes, social inequality, and abuses by local officials.
